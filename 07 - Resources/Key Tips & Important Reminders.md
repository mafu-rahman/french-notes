## 🔊 Pronunciation & Spelling

- **Silent letters are EVERYWHERE** in French — don't pronounce the last consonant unless it's C, R, F, or L ("CaReFuL")
- **H is always silent** (l'homme, heureux, hôtel)
- **E at the end is usually silent** (maison, monde, classe)
- **Single s between two vowel sounds usually sounds like z**: maison, rose
- **Double ss keeps the s sound**: poisson, classe
- **French nasal vowels** are tricky — **on/om**, **an/en**, **in/ain/ein**, **un** all have unique sounds
- Listen carefully to your instructor — your ear needs to learn the rhythms

## 📚 Grammar Essentials

### Gender is EVERYTHING
- **Every French noun has a gender** (masculine or feminine) — NO EXCEPTIONS
- Gender is **completely arbitrary** — "table" is feminine, "crayon" is masculine for no logical reason
- **The article shows the gender**: 
  - [[Definite Articles|le/la/les]], [[Indefinite Articles|un/une/des]]
  - Always learn a noun WITH its article

### Adjectives Come AFTER the Noun
- **English**: "the **big** house" (adjective first)
- **French**: "la maison **grande**" (noun first, adjective after)
- **Exception rule**: BAGS adjectives come before the noun:
  - **B**eauty: beau, joli
  - **A**ge: jeune, vieux, nouveau
  - **G**oodness: bon, mauvais, meilleur
  - **S**ize: grand, petit, long

### Articles & Contractions
- **[[Definite Articles]]**: le (m), la (f), l' (vowel), les (plural)
- **[[Indefinite Articles]]**: un (m), une (f), des (plural)
- **[[Contracted Articles]]**: 
  - le + à = **au** (à le = au)
  - le + de = **du** (de le = du)
  - les + à = **aux** (à les = aux)
  - les + de = **des** (de les = des)

## 🔤 Verbs: The Foundation

### Master These First
1. **[[ÊTRE]] (to be)** — IRREGULAR, essential, used everywhere
2. **[[AVOIR]] (to have)** — IRREGULAR, essential, used in past tense
3. **[[ALLER]] (to go)** — IRREGULAR, high frequency
4. **Regular verbs** — Once you know the patterns, most verbs follow them

### Irregular Verbs Don't Follow Rules
- **Don't try to guess** — you must memorize: [[ÊTRE]], [[AVOIR]], [[ALLER]], [[FAIRE]]
- These 4 are the most common; learn them cold before worrying about other irregulars

### Present Tense Verb Endings (Regular Verbs)

| Ending    | -ER (parler) | -IR (finir) | -RE (vendre) |
| --------- | ------------ | ----------- | ------------ |
| je        | **-e**       | **-is**     | **-s**       |
| tu        | **-es**      | **-is**     | **-s**       |
| il/elle   | **-e**       | **-it**     | **-**        |
| nous      | **-ons**     | **-issons** | **-ons**     |
| vous      | **-ez**      | **-issez**  | **-ez**      |
| ils/elles | **-ent**     | **-issent** | **-ent**     |

## 🏠 ÊTRE vs AVOIR

### Use ÊTRE When:
- Describing a **state or characteristic**: Je **suis** intelligent (I am intelligent)
- Describing a **location**: Je **suis** à Paris (I am in Paris)
- With certain verbs in [[Passé Composé]] (motion verbs like aller, venir, arriver)

### Use AVOIR When:
- Describing **possession**: J'**ai** un chat (I have a cat)
- Describing **age**: J'**ai** 20 ans (I am 20 years old)
- Most verbs in [[Passé Composé]]

### Remember
- "Je suis à Paris" ≠ "Je suis Paris" — ALWAYS use a preposition (à, en, etc.) for location
- "Je m'appelle..." ≠ "Je suis appelle" — use the reflexive verb for names

## ⌛ Passé Composé (Past Tense)

### The Formula
**Passé Composé = Auxiliary Verb (AVOIR or ÊTRE) + Past Participle**

### Most Verbs Use AVOIR
- J'**ai** parlé (I spoke)
- Tu **as** mangé (You ate)
- Il **a** bu (He drank)

### Common Verbs Use ÊTRE
**Remember: Dr. & Mrs. Vandertramp**
- devenir, revenir, venir
- aller, arriver, partir, entrer, sortir
- monter, descendre, rentrer, retourner, rester
- naître, mourir, tomber

**Tip**: These are mostly motion verbs (go, come, leave, arrive) or reflexive verbs

### Common Mistakes
- ❌ "Je ai parlé" → ✅ "J'**ai** parlé" (elision: ai becomes a')
- ❌ "Ils ont alleés" → ✅ "Ils **sont allés**" (aller uses être!)
- ❌ "Elle a venue" → ✅ "Elle **est venue**" (venir uses être!)

## 💬 Conversation & Questions

### Three Ways to Ask a Question

1. **Intonation** (casual, friendly) — Just raise your voice
   - Tu aimes le français? (With rising tone)

2. **Est-ce que** (neutral, works always)
   - **Est-ce que** tu aimes le français?

3. **Inversion** (formal) — Reverse subject & verb
   - Aimes-tu le français?
   - *Use this only when confident*

### Essential Questions Words
- **Où?** (Where) → Où es-tu?
- **Quand?** (When) → Quand arrivent-ils?
- **Comment?** (How) → Comment ça va?
- **Pourquoi?** (Why) → Pourquoi tu ris?
- **Qui?** (Who) → Qui est-ce?
- **Quoi?** (What) → De quoi parlez-vous?
- **Quel?** (Which) → Quel jour préfères-tu?

## 🎯 Common Traps & Mistakes

### Location Traps
- ❌ "Je suis Paris" → ✅ "Je suis **à** Paris" (add preposition!)
- ❌ "Je suis France" → ✅ "Je suis **en** France" (countries need prepositions)
- ❌ "Je suis école" → ✅ "Je suis **à** l'école" (à + definite article)

### Name Traps
- ❌ "Je suis appelle Marie" → ✅ "Je **m'appelle** Marie" (reflexive verb!)
- ✅ "Je suis français" → Correct (être for nationality)

### Verb Conjugation Traps
- ❌ "Nous va" → ✅ "Nous **allons**" (irregular!)
- ❌ "Ils sommes" → ✅ "Ils **sont**" (match the subject!)
- ❌ "Tu suis" → ✅ "Tu **es**" (être, not suivre!)

### Gender/Number Traps
- ❌ "La crayon" → ✅ "**Le** crayon" (crayon is masculine)
- ❌ "Un fille" → ✅ "**Une** fille" (fille is feminine)
- ❌ "Des maison" → ✅ "**Des maisons**" (plural requires -s)

## 📈 Learning Priorities

### Phase 1: Master Pronunciation & Articles
1. Learn [[01 - Fundamentals|French sounds]]
2. Learn [[Definite Articles]] & [[Indefinite Articles]]
3. Practice saying nouns WITH their articles

### Phase 2: Master Essential Verbs
1. Master [[ÊTRE]] conjugation (suis, es, est, sommes, êtes, sont)
2. Master [[AVOIR]] conjugation (ai, as, a, avons, avez, ont)
3. Master [[ALLER]] conjugation (vais, vas, va, allons, allez, vont)

### Phase 3: Build Vocabulary & Conversation
1. Numbers → Time → Colors (common theme vocabulary)
2. Question patterns & common dialogues
3. Regular verb patterns

### Phase 4: Add Complexity
1. [[Passé Composé]] (past tense)
2. Conditional & Imperative moods
3. More irregular verbs as needed
