## 🎯 Key Tips: The Tricky Ones

### 70-90 Are Weird!
French uses old counting:
- **70** = soixante-dix = "sixty-ten"
- **80** = quatre-vingts = "four-twenties"
- **90** = quatre-vingt-dix = "four-twenties-ten"

This is just how it is—memorize it!

### 81 & 91 Are Special
- **81** = quatre-vingt-et-un (has "et" because -vingt is emphasized)
- **91** = quatre-vingt-onze (NO "et")

### Agreement With Vingt & Cent
- **20**: **vingt** (no 's')
- **200**: **deux cents** (WITH 's' because multiplied)
- **210**: **deux cent dix** (NO 's' because followed by other number)
- **100**: **cent** (no 's')
- **300**: **trois cents** (WITH 's' because multiplied)
- **310**: **trois cent dix** (NO 's')

## 🗣️ Pronunciation Tricks

### Silent Letters
- **six** = "sees" (final x is silent EXCEPT before vowels: six enfants = "seez-zon-fahnt")
- **dix** = "dees" (final x is silent EXCEPT before vowels: dix enfants = "deez-zon-fahnt")
- **cent** = "sahn" (t is silent)
- **vingt** = "vang" (t is silent)
- **neuf** = "nuh" (final f sounds like 'v' before heures: neuf heures = "nuv-uhr")

### Liaison (Extra Sound)
When a number ending in a consonant sound is followed by a vowel, you pronounce the final letter:
- **six** (alone) = "sees" → **six enfants** = "seez-zon-fahnt"
- **dix** (alone) = "dees" → **dix enfants** = "deez-zon-fahnt"
- **neuf** (alone) = "nuh" → **neuf heures** = "nuv-uhr"

## 💬 Using Numbers in Sentences

### Age
- J'**ai** vingt et un ans. (I am 21 years old.)
- Tu **as** quarante ans. (You are 40 years old.)

### Quantity
- J'**ai** trois livres. (I have three books.)
- Il y **a** cinq enfants. (There are five children.)

### Time
- Il **est** dix heures. (It is 10 o'clock.)
- Nous **allons** à midi. (We are going at noon.)

### Price
- Ça coûte vingt euros. (It costs 20 euros.)
- Les pommes coûtent deux euros le kilo. (Apples cost 2 euros per kilo.)

### Phone/Address
- Mon numéro est zéro-six-quarante-deux... (My number is 06-42...)
