# 🔊 Pronunciation Guide

French pronunciation is challenging for English speakers, but patterns exist. Master these sounds early.

## 🔤 Basic Rules

### Silent Letters (The Big One)
- **Most final consonants are SILENT** — don't pronounce the last letter unless it's C, R, F, or L ("CaReFuL")
  - "Paris" = pa-**REE** (s is silent, r is pronounced)
  - "vous" = VOO (s is silent)
  - "français" = frahn-**SAY** (s at end IS pronounced because... French)
  
### H is Always Silent
- "hôtel" = oh-**TEL**
- "l'homme" = **LOM** (h is silent, so elision occurs)
- "heureux" = u-**RUH**

### E at the End is Usually Silent
- "maison" = may-**ZON** (final e silent)
- "classe" = **KLASS** (final e silent)
- "table" = **TAB** (final e silent)

### Consonants
- **C** before e, i = soft **S** sound (ceci = say-**SEE**)
- **C** before a, o, u = hard **K** sound (ca = **KA**)
- **G** before e, i = soft **ZH** sound (je = **ZHuh**)
- **G** before a, o, u = hard **G** sound (gare = **GAR**)

## 🔊 French Vowels & Nasal Vowels

French vowels are different from English—your mouth position matters.

### Pure Vowels

| Letter | Sound     | Example        | How to Say It                |
| ------ | --------- | -------------- | ---------------------------- |
| **a**  | AH (open) | "chat" (cat)   | Open mouth like saying "ah!" |
| **e**  | AY        | "été" (summer) | Smile, say "ay"              |
| **i**  | EE        | "oui" (yes)    | Say "ee" with rounded lips   |
| **o**  | OH        | "eau" (water)  | Rounded lips, "oh"           |
| **u**  | OO        | "tu" (you)     | Lips very rounded, "oo"      |
| **y**  | EE        | "y"            | Same as "i"                  |

### The Tricky Ones: Nasal Vowels

These don't exist in English. The sound comes through your **nose**, not just your mouth.

| Nasal | Spelling | Sound | Example |
|-------|----------|-------|---------|
| **ã** | an, en, am, em | ONGGG (nasal a) | "dans" (in), "enfant" (child) |
| **ẽ** | in, im, ein, ain, ien, aim | UNG (nasal e) | "vin" (wine), "faim" (hunger) |
| **õ** | on, om | ONG (nasal o) | "bon" (good), "mon" (my) |
| **ũ** | un, um | UNG (nasal u) | "un" (one), "parfum" (perfume) |

**Pro Tip**: Say "on" (the English word), then pinch your nose—that nasal twang is the sound!

## 🎵 Rhythm & Intonation

### Stress & Syllables
- French **doesn't stress syllables** the way English does
- Pronounce each syllable evenly
- Stress falls at the END of a phrase or word group, not at the beginning

**English**: **PREfecture** (stress first syllable)  
**French**: pre-fek-**TOOR** (stress last syllable)

### Rising & Falling Tones
- Statements **end with falling tone**: "Je suis français." ↘️
- Yes/no questions **end with rising tone**: "Tu es français?" ↗️
- Listen to your instructor to develop this instinct

| Word | ❌ Wrong | ✅ Correct |
|------|---------|-----------|
| **très** (very) | TREZ | **TREH** |
| **trois** (three) | TWAH | **TWAH** (both same, but say "ah") |
| **Métro** | MET-roh | **MEH-TROH** |
| **croissant** | cruh-**SANT** | **KRAH-sahn** |
| **oui** (yes) | OO-ee | **WEE** |
| **noir** (black) | NWAH | **NWAHR** |

## 🧠 Practice Strategy

1. **Listen actively** to your instructor and native speakers
2. **Record yourself** speaking and compare to native audio
3. **Exaggerate mouth movements** initially—you're learning new positions
4. **Don't worry about perfection** early—French speakers will understand you
5. **Focus on nasal vowels** and silent letters first—those trip up most English speakers
