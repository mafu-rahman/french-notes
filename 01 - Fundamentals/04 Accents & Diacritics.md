# Accents and Diacritics

French accents are part of spelling. Sometimes they change pronunciation, sometimes they distinguish words, and sometimes they show older spelling history.

Do not treat accents as decoration. Learn them with the word.

---

## The Main Accents

| Mark | Name | Example | Beginner Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| é | accent aigu | été | often sounds like "ay" |
| è | accent grave | mère | often sounds like "eh" |
| ê | accent circonflexe | forêt | often sounds like "eh"; sometimes historical |
| ë | tréma | Noël | pronounce the vowel separately |
| ç | cédille | français | makes c sound like s |
| à / ù | accent grave | à, où | often distinguishes words |

---

## Accent Aigu: é

The **accent aigu** only appears on **e**: **é**.

It usually has a closed "ay" sound.

Examples:

- été = summer
- café = coffee / cafe
- école = school
- répéter = to repeat

Common beginner pattern:

- **-er** infinitives often end with a sound like **é**, but they are spelled without the accent: parler, aimer, étudier
- **-é** is common in past participles: parlé, aimé, étudié

---

## Accent Grave: è, à, ù

### è

The **è** usually has a more open "eh" sound.

Examples:

- mère = mother
- père = father
- frère = brother
- très = very

### à

The accent distinguishes **à** from **a**.

| Word | Meaning |
|---|---|
| a | has |
| à | to / at / in |

Examples:

- Il **a** un livre. = He has a book.
- Il va **à** Paris. = He is going to Paris.

### où

The accent distinguishes **où** from **ou**.

| Word | Meaning |
|---|---|
| ou | or |
| où | where |

Examples:

- Thé **ou** café ? = Tea or coffee?
- **Où** habites-tu ? = Where do you live?

---

## Accent Circonflexe: â, ê, î, ô, û

The **accent circonflexe** can appear on several vowels.

Examples:

- être = to be
- fenêtre = window
- forêt = forest
- âge = age
- sûr = sure

It sometimes shows that an old **s** disappeared:

- forêt is related to forest
- hôpital is related to hospital

As a beginner, the main thing is to memorize it as part of the word.

---

## Tréma: ë, ï, ü

The **tréma** tells you to pronounce two vowels separately instead of combining them.

Examples:

- Noël = Christmas
- naïf = naive
- Haïti = Haiti

Without the tréma, you might try to combine the vowel sounds.

---

## Cédille: ç

The **cédille** appears under **c**: **ç**.

It makes **c** sound like **s** before **a**, **o**, or **u**.

Examples:

- français = French
- garçon = boy
- leçon = lesson
- ça = that / it

Compare:

| Without ç | Sound | With ç | Sound |
|---|---|---|---|
| ca | ka | ça | sa |
| co | ko | ço | so |
| cu | ku | çu | su |

---

## Words Where Accents Change Meaning

| Without Accent | Meaning | With Accent | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| a | has | à | to / at |
| ou | or | où | where |
| sur | on | sûr | sure |
| du | of the / some | dû | owed / had to |

---

## Typing Accents

On a Mac, hold the letter key and choose the accented version.

Examples:

- hold `e` -> é, è, ê, ë
- hold `a` -> à, â
- hold `c` -> ç

---

## Common Mistakes

❌ cafe  
✅ café

❌ ou habites-tu ?  
✅ où habites-tu ?

❌ Il va a Paris.  
✅ Il va à Paris.

❌ francais  
✅ français
