# 🗣️ Subject Pronouns

Subject pronouns replace nouns as the subject of a verb. Master these—they're essential for every sentence.

---

## 📋 The Six Subject Pronouns

| Pronoun   | English                         | Pronunciation | Notes                                                |
| --------- | ------------------------------- | ------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- |
| **je**    | I                               | zhuh          | Becomes **j'** before vowels                         |
| **tu**    | you (singular, informal)        | too           | Use with friends, family, people your age            |
| **il**    | he / it                         | eel           | For masculine nouns (le, un)                         |
| **elle**  | she / it                        | ell           | For feminine nouns (la, une)                         |
| **on**    | one / we (informal)             | ohn           | Means "one" literally, but often means "we" casually |
| **nous**  | we                              | noo           | Formal "we," or group including speaker              |
| **vous**  | you (plural/formal)             | voo           | Polite singular OR plural "you"                      |
| **ils**   | they (masculine or mixed group) | eel           | For masculine plural nouns (les) or mixed genders    |
| **elles** | they (all feminine)             | ell           | For all-feminine plural nouns (les)                  |

---

## 🎯 Examples

### Je (I)
- **Je** suis français. (I am French.)
- **Je** aime les livres. (I like books.) — Note: becomes **j'aime**

### Tu (You - Singular Informal)
- **Tu** es intelligent. (You are intelligent.)
- **Tu** aimes le café? (Do you like coffee?)

### Il (He / It)
- **Il** est médecin. (He is a doctor.)
- **Il** vient de Paris. (He comes from Paris.)

### Elle (She / It)
- **Elle** est professeur. (She is a teacher.)
- **Elle** aime danser. (She likes to dance.)

### On (One / We - Informal)
- **On** parle français. (We speak French.) OR (One speaks French.)
- **On** va au café? (Shall we go to the café?)
- **On** est contents. (We are happy.) — Grammatically, "on" is singular, but in reality it's used like plural

### Nous (We - Formal/Group)
- **Nous** sommes français. (We are French.)
- **Nous** allons à l'école. (We go to school.)

### Vous (You - Plural or Formal Singular)
- **Vous** êtes professeur? (Are you a teacher?) — FORMAL/RESPECTFUL
- **Vous** êtes intelligents? (Are you intelligent?) — PLURAL

### Ils (They - Masculine/Mixed)
- **Ils** sont médecins. (They are doctors.) — All male or mixed group
- **Ils** viennent de Paris. (They come from Paris.)

### Elles (They - All Feminine)
- **Elles** sont professeurs. (They are teachers.) — All female
- **Elles** aiment danser. (They like to dance.)

## 🎯 Key Tips

### Tu vs. Vous
This is crucial for French culture:

| Situation | Use |
|-----------|-----|
| Talking to a friend, family member, or peer | **Tu** |
| Talking to a stranger, elder, boss, or in formal settings | **Vous** |
| Talking to multiple people | **Vous** |
| Talking to a child you know well | **Tu** |

**Example scenarios:**
- ✅ "Tu aimes le café?" (friend)
- ✅ "Vous aimez le café?" (boss/stranger/formal)
- ✅ "Vous aimez le café?" (group of friends together)

### On = We (Casual)
- In formal French: **on** means "one" (impersonal)
- In casual French: **on** is used instead of **nous** all the time
- **"On va au café?"** is more natural than "Nous allons au café?"
- Grammatically, **on** takes singular verb forms, but in reality it's plural

### Gender of Pronouns
- **Il** and **Elle** refer to the gender of the noun, not the person
- **Le professeur** → **Il** (even if it's a woman, because "professeur" is masculine)
- **La professeur** (can exist) → **Elle**
- In modern French, women can use "professeure" or "professeur" (masculine remains common)

### Ils vs. Elles
- **Ils** = masculine plural OR mixed group (even 1 male + 10 females = "ils")
- **Elles** = all feminine

## 💬 Verb Conjugation by Pronoun

Here's how verbs change with each pronoun (using ÊTRE as example):

| Pronoun | ÊTRE | Example |
|---------|------|---------|
| Je | suis | Je **suis** français |
| Tu | es | Tu **es** français |
| Il/Elle | est | Il **est** français |
| On | est | On **est** français |
| Nous | sommes | Nous **sommes** français |
| Vous | êtes | Vous **êtes** français |
| Ils/Elles | sont | Ils **sont** français |

## 🗣️ Common Mistakes

### Mistake 1: Using Tu in Formal Situations
❌ "Tu es professeur?" (to your boss) → ✅ "**Vous** êtes professeur?"

### Mistake 2: Using Vous for Your Friend
❌ "Vous aimez le café?" (to your best friend) → ✅ "**Tu** aimes le café?"

### Mistake 3: Forgetting Elision With je
❌ "Je ai faim" → ✅ "**J'** ai faim"
❌ "Je aime" → ✅ "**J'** aime"

### Mistake 4: Mixing Pronoun Gender With Speaker Gender
- If you're a woman saying "I am French": **Je suis française** (the adjective changes, but the pronoun "je" stays the same)
- Pronouns don't change with speaker gender—only the adjective does

### Mistake 5: Using "On" Incorrectly
❌ "On suis" → ✅ "**On est**" (on takes singular verb form)
❌ "On va au cinéma?" → ✅ "**On va** au cinéma?" (correct—on = singular verb)

## 📝 Practice: Choose the Right Pronoun

1. You (formal, to a teacher): ___ êtes professeur? → **Vous**
2. I am French: ___ suis français → **Je**
3. They (all female) are happy: ___ sont contentes → **Elles**
4. We (casual) are going: ___ allons au café → **On**
5. He is a doctor: ___ est médecin → **Il**
6. You (plural) like coffee: ___ aimez le café → **Vous**
7. She likes to dance: ___ aime danser → **Elle**
